# Hablito curriculum: A1 → C1

Thirty downloadable levels that take a learner from their first words to fluent,
natural conversation in Latin American (Mexican) Spanish.

## How the curriculum was designed

- **Framework.** Levels follow the Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR)
  bands A1 → C1. B2 is where learners can talk with native speakers "with a degree
  of fluency and spontaneity… without strain for either party"; C1 adds fluent,
  spontaneous expression "without much obvious searching for expressions". Those
  two descriptors are the targets of stages 4 and 5.
- **Grammar sequence.** It follows the usual order of Spanish-as-a-foreign-language
  syllabi based on the Instituto Cervantes *Plan Curricular* (*Niveles de
  Referencia*): present tense and *ser/estar* first, then the past tenses, the future
  and conditional, the subjunctive (introduced at B1, built up through B2), and
  hypotheticals, concessions and register at B2–C1.
- **Speaking first.** Each level teaches one grammar focus through two short phrase
  lessons, then uses it in a real-life conversation. Every lesson has an automatic
  quiz, and mistakes become Review cards.
- **Regional variety.** Vocabulary and usage are Mexican: *departamento*, *¿Bueno?*,
  *ahorita*, *¡Qué padre!*. Slang appears only at C1, with a note on register.

## Stages

| Stage | Levels | CEFR | The learner can… |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Survival | 1–4 | A1 | Greet, say how they feel, tell the time, order food, shop and pay. |
| 2. Everyday life | 5–8 | A2 | Get around town, talk about family and home, see a doctor, chat at work. |
| 3. Independent | 9–16 | B1 | Solve travel problems, tell past stories, make plans, describe people, handle calls, rent a home, express feelings. |
| 4. Confident | 17–24 | B2 | Give advice, interview for a job, complain and negotiate, talk hypotheticals, discuss news and culture, debate, tell vivid stories. |
| 5. Fluent | 25–30 | C1 | Persuade, use idioms and slang, run formal meetings and emails, show tact, discuss abstract topics, keep a conversation flowing naturally. |

## Size today, and where to grow

Each level currently has 2 lessons × 6 phrases plus 1 conversation of 3 turns: 360
phrases and 30 conversations in total. That is a strong spine, but real fluency
needs more practice volume. The suggested next steps, in order:

1. **Native review.** Have a Mexican Spanish speaker check every pack, and a
   Vietnamese speaker check the `vi` text, before release.
2. **More conversations per level.** Add 2–3 more scenarios per level using the same
   grammar focus. This is the biggest gain for speaking practice.
3. **Bigger lessons at B2–C1.** Move these lessons toward 10–12 phrases.
4. **C2 / mastery (optional).** Regional varieties (Spain, Argentina, Colombia),
   literature and media, and professional specializations (medical, legal,
   business).

## Editing the content

- **The pack JSON files in `packs/` are what ships.** Edit them directly, then run
  `node content/courses/build-manifest.mjs` (or `bash content/courses/publish.sh`).
- **`source/` holds the Python used to draft all 30 levels.** It is only an
  authoring aid. Running it again overwrites `packs/`, so either keep editing the
  Python and regenerate, or edit the JSON directly. Don't mix the two.

  ```bash
  python3 content/courses/source/generate.py content/courses/packs \
    content/courses/source/levels_11_15.py content/courses/source/levels_16_20.py \
    content/courses/source/levels_21_25.py content/courses/source/levels_26_30.py
  ```

- **Publishing a change.** Raise a level's `"version"` to publish a change to it;
  learners who downloaded it see "Update available".
